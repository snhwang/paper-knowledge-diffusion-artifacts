# Privileged and confidential — legal assessment, incident XYZ-2300

**Author:** Legal and compliance counsel (Marta Lindqvist)
**Prepared for:** Incident commander.

## Exposure

Approximately 27,000 individuals' phone numbers, first names, clinic names and appointment times were in message logs accessed at our SMS vendor. That is a personal data breach. XYZ processes this data on behalf of the 88 clinics, who are the controllers; XYZ's own duty is to the clinics, and the clinics decide on regulator and patient notification.

## Obligations

- To the clinics: our processing terms (clause 8.3) require notice to each controller within 48 hours of XYZ becoming aware of a breach at XYZ or its sub-processor. We became aware at 15:10 Monday; notice to all 88 clinics is due by 15:10 Wednesday.
- To regulators and patients: none directly. We must give the clinics what they need to decide: counts per clinic, the data elements, and the window.
- Vendor contract: Relay's contract (clause 14) caps its liability at 12 months' fees, about $96,000, and requires Relay to indemnify XYZ for breach costs up to that cap. We should put Relay on notice of a claim this week to preserve it.

## What may and may not be stated externally

- Do not name Relay publicly until Relay has made its own statement; refer to "a third-party messaging provider".
- Do not give patient counts publicly; give each clinic its own count privately.
- Do not state that no XYZ system was compromised as though it settles the matter; the data was ours to protect wherever it was.
- It may be stated that reminder text messages are paused because of a security incident at a third-party provider, that no XYZ system was accessed, and that clinics have been given details.

## Recommendation

Notify all 88 clinics by Wednesday 15:10 with per-clinic counts; put Relay on notice under clause 14; keep SMS off until the attestation is in hand.
