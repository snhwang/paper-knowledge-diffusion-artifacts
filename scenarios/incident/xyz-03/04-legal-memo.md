# Privileged and confidential — legal assessment, incident XYZ-2202

**Author:** Legal and compliance counsel (Marta Lindqvist)
**Prepared for:** Incident commander. Employment matters below are not to be discussed outside incident leadership and HR.

## Exposure

1,880 business contact records (names, work emails, work phones, account tier) for 212 customers were removed by a departing employee. This is personal data of clinic staff but not patient data, and the likely harm is unsolicited contact rather than fraud. My assessment is that regulator notification is not required in either district: the risk to individuals is low. The decision and its reasoning must be documented (Northern District § 47(2)).

## Contractual obligations

- Three customers — Harbor Clinic Group, Westfield Family Practice and Lakeside Paediatrics — have contracts requiring notice of any unauthorised access to their data within 7 days of discovery (clause 9.1). Discovery is Friday 08:50; notice is due by next Friday 08:50.
- All other customers: no contractual notice obligation, but I recommend a courtesy notice to all 212 on Monday, which also reduces the risk of a complaint.

## Employment matter

The employee's contract carries a confidentiality obligation (clause 12) that survives termination. Recommended: a letter from outside counsel on Monday demanding deletion of the file and written certification within 5 business days, and reserving the right to seek an injunction. Do not contact the former employee directly and do not accuse them of anything to customers.

## What may and may not be stated externally

- Do not name or describe the former employee, their role or their departure.
- Do not state the number of records or the file name.
- It may be stated that a former employee's access was reviewed, that a list of customer business contact details was exported without authorisation, that no patient data was involved, and that customers may receive unsolicited contact.
